# SupportBot Studio — Pricing & Packaging

> Internal sales reference. Use this on calls, in proposals, and during onboarding.
> Confidence is the product — quote prices flat, no hedging.

## 1. Pricing Tiers

### Starter — $99 setup + $49/month

For small businesses testing AI support for the first time.

- 1 AI agent
- Up to 500 conversations/month
- Email escalation
- Basic analytics
- 5-day setup

### Growth — $199 setup + $99/month

The default recommendation. Most clients land here.

- 1 AI agent
- Up to 2,000 conversations/month
- Email + Telegram escalation
- Full analytics + reports
- Brand Voice DNA
- 3-day setup

### Enterprise — Custom pricing

Larger teams with multiple departments, products, or compliance needs.

- Multiple AI agents
- Unlimited conversations
- All features
- Priority support
- Custom integrations
- Dedicated onboarding

> **Pricing rule of thumb:** if a prospect asks "which one is right for us?" the answer is Growth, unless conversation volume genuinely sits below 500/month. The features in Growth (Brand Voice DNA + Telegram escalation) are the difference between "we tried a chatbot" and "this feels like our team."

## 2. What's Included

### What the setup fee covers (one-time)

- Installation of the bot on the client's website
- Configuration of the AI agent (personality, scope, fallback rules)
- Knowledge base build — FAQs, products, services, policies fed into the agent
- Branding (colors, logo, name, tone of voice)
- End-to-end testing on staging before go-live
- Go-live and post-launch monitoring for the first 48 hours

### What the monthly fee covers (recurring)

- Hosting on dedicated infrastructure
- Maintenance and uptime monitoring
- Software updates and new features (rolled out automatically)
- Email/chat support from Kay
- All Claude AI usage costs within the conversation cap

> **Important:** the monthly fee absorbs Claude API costs. The client never sees a separate AI bill. If they exceed their conversation cap, you bill the overage at the next tier's rate or move them up a plan.

## 3. Onboarding Process

Day-by-day flow from signed contract to live bot:

| Day | What Kay does | What client provides |
|---|---|---|
| **Day 0** — kickoff | Send welcome email + intake form | Sign contract, pay setup fee |
| **Day 1** — discovery | Review intake, draft AI personality | FAQs, products, escalation contacts, website access |
| **Day 2** — build | Configure agent, load knowledge base, apply branding | Brand assets (logo, colors), tone of voice samples |
| **Day 3** — test | Run scripted + real-world test conversations | Review test transcript, flag corrections |
| **Day 4** — staging review | Send staging link, walk client through admin panel | Approve or request changes |
| **Day 5** — go-live | Push to production, monitor first conversations | Announce to customers (optional) |

For Starter: same steps, slower pace (5 days). For Growth: 3-day track. For Enterprise: scoped per project, typically 2–4 weeks.

> Always overdeliver on Day 5. A client who sees their bot answer a real customer correctly on launch day will renew for years.

## 4. What Kay Needs From Client to Start

Send this list as a single intake form. Don't make them guess.

1. **Business info**
   - Legal business name + trading name
   - One-line description of what they sell
   - Top 3 products or services
2. **Branding assets**
   - Logo (SVG or high-res PNG)
   - Primary brand color (hex)
   - Tone preference: friendly / professional / formal
3. **FAQs**
   - 10–30 most common customer questions with answers
   - Existing help docs, policies, or knowledge base (any format)
4. **Escalation contacts**
   - Email address(es) for escalations
   - Telegram username and/or WhatsApp number (Growth and Enterprise)
   - Office hours (when humans are available)
5. **Website access**
   - URL of the live site
   - One of: developer contact who can paste a script tag, OR CMS access (Shopify/Wix/WordPress admin), OR a 15-minute screenshare to install together

> If the client can't supply FAQs, run a 30-minute discovery call and write them yourself. Bill it inside the setup fee — don't itemise.

## 5. Common Objections + Responses

### "It's too expensive."

> "Compared to what? One support agent costs you 30–50× this every month, works 8 hours a day, and forgets every conversation. The bot works 24/7, never quits, and gets better every week. The Starter plan pays for itself the first time it answers a question at 2am that would otherwise become a refund request."

If they push: offer Starter as a low-risk entry point. Don't discount Growth — drop them down a tier instead.

### "We already have live chat."

> "Live chat is a channel. SupportBot is the agent that handles 80% of conversations on that channel without your team. You can keep your live chat — we replace the queue, not the inbox."

Position SupportBot as augmenting, not replacing, their existing tools.

### "What if the AI gives wrong answers?"

> "It only answers from the knowledge base you give us. Anything outside that scope, it escalates to you on Telegram or email. We test every agent against 50+ scripted questions before launch — and you see every conversation in the analytics dashboard, so corrections happen in hours, not weeks."

This is the #1 fear. Lean into transparency: show them the analytics screen, show them the escalation flow.

### "Is my customer data safe?"

> "Conversations live on dedicated infrastructure. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest. We don't sell, share, or train models on your data. If you ever cancel, we export your data and wipe our copy within 30 days."

Have a one-page data handling sheet ready to send. Enterprise prospects will ask for it in writing.

### "Can we cancel anytime?"

> "Yes. Monthly billing, 30-day notice, no lock-in. Setup fee is non-refundable once work has started — but the monthly fee stops the moment you decide to leave."

Never use cancellation as a sales tactic. Confidence in the product means confidence they'll stay.

### "How is this different from Intercom/Zendesk/HubSpot?"

> "Those are help-desk platforms — you pay per seat for your team to handle tickets. SupportBot replaces the team for the 80% of questions that don't need a human. We're not competing with Intercom — we plug into it, or sit alongside it, and reduce the volume your team has to handle."

If they're already paying $500+/month for Intercom, SupportBot is an obvious add-on, not a replacement.

## 6. Upsell Opportunities

Order matters — pitch these only after the bot is live and performing.

### Brand Voice DNA — included in Growth, available as +$50/month upgrade for Starter
The bot writes in the client's actual voice — sampled from their existing emails, website copy, social posts. This is what turns "a chatbot" into "our chatbot." Premium feel, low effort to deliver.

### Additional AI agents — +$49/month per extra agent
Sell when a client has multiple products, departments, or languages. Examples: separate agent for sales vs. support, or one per product line. Each agent has its own knowledge base and personality.

### Webhook integrations — +$99 one-time per integration
Slack, Discord, CRM systems, custom internal tools. Quote per integration based on complexity. Simple webhook (post message to channel) = $99. Bidirectional integration with state = $299–$499.

### Custom features — quoted separately
Anything outside the standard product. Common asks: lead-scoring logic, custom analytics dashboards, multi-language reports, white-label admin panel. Always scope and quote in writing before starting.

> Upsell timing: wait until month 2 or 3, after the client has seen real ROI. Pitching upgrades on the kickoff call kills trust.

## 7. Payment Terms

- **Setup fee:** 100% upfront before any work begins. No exceptions.
- **Monthly fee:** billed monthly in advance, on the same calendar day as go-live.
- **Accepted methods:** PayPal, Wise, bank transfer. Crypto (USDT/USDC) on request for clients who prefer it.
- **Refunds:** no refunds on setup fee once work has started. Monthly fees are non-refundable for the current month but stop immediately on cancellation.
- **Late payments:** service paused after 7 days overdue, fully suspended after 14 days. Reinstatement requires payment in full plus a $25 reactivation fee.
- **Currency:** all pricing in USD. Clients pay the USD-equivalent in their local currency at the prevailing rate on invoice date.

> Never start work on a verbal agreement. Signed contract + setup fee paid = work begins. This protects you and signals professionalism to the client.

## 8. Enterprise Negotiation Guide

### How to scope custom projects

Enterprise pricing is custom because the work is custom. Don't quote on the first call. Run a discovery process:

1. **Discovery call (60 minutes).** Map their current support stack, conversation volume, integrations, and pain points. Take notes — you'll quote from these.
2. **Written scope (within 3 business days).** Send a one-page scope: what's in, what's out, timeline, total price. No itemised costs — quote a single number.
3. **Walkthrough call (30 minutes).** Walk them through the scope. Answer questions. Get verbal commitment.
4. **Contract + 50% deposit.** Send contract. 50% upfront for enterprise (vs. 100% for Starter/Growth — the larger ticket justifies the split).

Typical enterprise projects land between $2,000 and $10,000 setup, with $300–$800/month recurring depending on volume and number of agents.

### When to offer discounts

Default position: don't discount. The price is the price. But there are two acceptable discount levers:

- **Annual prepay = 2 months free.** A client paying 12 months upfront gets months 11 and 12 free. This is a cash-flow win for you and a real saving for them.
- **Multi-agent volume discount.** 3+ agents on Enterprise: 10% off recurring. 5+ agents: 15% off recurring. Setup fees are never discounted.

Never discount setup fees. The setup is real work, real hours. Discounting it signals the price wasn't real to begin with.

### Red flags to watch for

Walk away from any enterprise deal showing two or more of these:

- Wants to negotiate price before scope is agreed
- Refuses to sign a contract ("just send an invoice")
- Asks for custom legal terms beyond standard MSA
- Drags out discovery for more than 4 weeks without committing
- Asks for free trials, free POCs, or "we'll pay if it works"
- Requires non-standard payment terms (Net-60, Net-90)
- Insists on owning the underlying AI prompts/model weights
- Single decision-maker who keeps "checking with the team" but never schedules a joint call

The cost of a bad enterprise client is 10× a good one. Filtering hard upfront is the highest-leverage thing you can do.
